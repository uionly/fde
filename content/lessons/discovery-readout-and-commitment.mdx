## Discovery ends with a decision, not a document

A readout should let accountable stakeholders decide whether to proceed, revise, or stop. A chronology of interviews and workshops forces the audience to reconstruct the argument. Instead, connect evidence from the current workflow to a bounded commitment and make unresolved uncertainty explicit.

<CustomerScenario title="A prototype looking for a problem">
  Northstar's team finishes discovery with 70 slides, three prototype screens, and no agreed pilot. Operations expects reply drafting, security reviewed policy search, and the sponsor still speaks about autonomous resolution. Each group believes a different decision was made.
</CustomerScenario>

Structure the readout around a clear chain: affected users and current workflow; quantified problem and baseline; target outcome and guardrails; prioritized use case; proposed release boundary; known constraints; highest-risk assumptions; validation plan; operating owners; and decision requested.

Show evidence and confidence. Separate observations, interpretations, and commitments. If only twelve tickets were sampled, say so and explain what the sample can support. Include important counterevidence rather than smoothing it away to create apparent consensus.

## Create a two-sided commitment

The delivery team commits to a bounded outcome, validation gates, and transparent risk management. The customer commits named owners, user access, data, integration environments, review turnaround, and operational participation. Put dates on dependencies that control the schedule.

Define exit conditions before building. The team might stop or redesign if permission-aware retrieval cannot be demonstrated, representative answer quality stays below threshold, or the pilot adds handling time. Clear exits protect both parties from escalating commitment to a weak approach.

<Callout title="Close the meeting explicitly">
  Record the decision, scope, exclusions, owners, first validation gate, and next decision date. Send the record while disagreements are still easy to correct.
</Callout>

<FDEPrinciple>
  Discovery creates value when evidence becomes a shared, bounded, and reversible delivery commitment.
</FDEPrinciple>

The readout is also the bridge to architecture. It tells designers what outcome to optimize, which trust boundaries matter, what evidence must be produced, and which future possibilities should not inflate the first release.
