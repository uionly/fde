## Communication is part of the control system

In forward-deployed work, technical progress depends on customer decisions, access, reviews, and operational knowledge. Communication is therefore not a status ceremony. It is the mechanism that keeps decisions timely, assumptions visible, and risk owned by the right people.

<CustomerScenario title="Green status, red reality">
  Northstar's project report says engineering is on schedule. The security review has not started, policy owners disagree about exception handling, and production access needs a six-week lead time. None of those facts appear because the team reports only completed tickets.
</CustomerScenario>

Build a rhythm around audiences and decisions. A working session can resolve workflow details with specialists. A short weekly readout should show outcomes, evidence, decisions needed, risks, and next tests. An executive checkpoint should focus on tradeoffs requiring authority, not replay the engineering backlog.

Use concrete language. “Integration is at risk” does not help. “CRM sandbox access is due Friday; without it, we cannot validate write-back before the release decision on 18 May” identifies the dependency, consequence, and deadline. Pair each risk with an owner and next action.

## Show the work honestly

Demonstrations should expose the current boundary. Use representative customer cases, include a known failure, and label mocked integrations. A flawless scripted demo may earn applause while concealing the evidence needed for a safe decision.

<Callout title="Five-line field update">
  Outcome movement; evidence learned; decision required; active risk; next customer-visible test.
</Callout>

When disagreement appears, capture it as a decision record rather than letting it recur in every meeting. State the options, relevant evidence, decision owner, date, and consequences. Reopen the decision only when new evidence changes the premise.

<FDEPrinciple>
  Communicate at the cadence of customer decisions, with enough evidence that stakeholders can act.
</FDEPrinciple>

Credibility comes from making uncertainty legible. Early warnings with a recovery plan strengthen trust. Late surprises, even when technically explainable, weaken it. A reliable communication rhythm gives both teams permission to surface problems while they are still inexpensive to solve.
